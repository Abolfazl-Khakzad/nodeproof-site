The problem

11 September 2026

Since 11 September 2026, manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities in their products to ENISA within 24 hours.

11 December 2027

From 11 December 2027, every product with digital elements sold in the EU must meet the CRA to carry the CE mark.

Fines reach €15 million or 2.5% of worldwide turnover.

Most of the risk is not in your own code, but in the third-party libraries inside the firmware: components that often nobody tracks.

This applies to you if you make, or sell under your own brand:

  • HMI panels, PLCs, gateways and remote-service modules
  • sensors and IoT devices
  • machines and appliances with firmware and a connection (Wi-Fi, Ethernet, Bluetooth, app, cloud)

What we do

  1. Firmware analysis

    We build the SBOM (software bill of materials) and find known vulnerabilities in its components.

  2. CRA gap analysis

    We check the product against the Annex I essential requirements and tell you what is missing, in order of priority.

  3. Documentation

    We prepare the risk assessment, technical documentation and vulnerability handling policy.

  4. Ongoing monitoring

    Every month we check new vulnerabilities against your products and help you report to ENISA on time.

We explain everything in plain language: short, clear reports for management, technical detail for your developers.

FAQ

Does the CRA apply to a small company?
Yes. The CRA applies to every manufacturer, whatever its size, if the product has software and a data connection. Only a few sectors with their own rules are excluded, such as medical devices, vehicles and aviation.
Do we need to give you our source code?
No. The firmware file is enough for the analysis. If you have build files (for example Yocto or Buildroot), the results are even more precise.
Will our data stay confidential?
Yes. We sign an NDA before receiving any file, and we analyse firmware only with your written authorisation.
